In my systematic literature review (SLR), I focus on studies that use machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models to predict agitation in people living with dementia.

After selecting the relevant studies, I need to understand what these studies have in common, how they are different, and what important patterns can be found across them. 
Thematic analysis can help organise these findings into meaningful themes.

<!--more-->

### 1. What are themes?
A theme is an important idea or pattern that appears across the studies.

For example, suppose several studies use different machine learning models to predict agitation in people living with dementia. Some studies may use:

physiological data, such as heart rate;
behavioural data, such as movement or activity;
environmental data, such as noise;
clinical information, such as dementia severity or medication;
multimodal data that combine several types of information.

These repeated patterns can be grouped into themes.

For example, one possible theme could be:

**Theme 1: Different data sources are used to predict agitation**

Under this theme, we may find that researchers use physiological, behavioural, clinical, and environmental information as input features for their prediction models.

A theme is more than simply a topic. It should explain an important pattern or idea across the studies.

For example:

Topic: Machine learning models
Theme: Machine learning models use different types of patient and environmental data to predict agitation.

The second example tells us more about what we found in the literature.

### 2. Why do we need to analyse themes?
An SLR can include many studies. Each study may use a different dataset, prediction model, feature set, evaluation method, and outcome definition.

If we only describe each study one by one, it can be difficult to see the bigger picture.

Thematic analysis helps us move from individual studies to common patterns across the literature.

It can help us answer questions such as:

- What types of data are commonly used?
- What machine learning and deep learning models have been used?
- What features are used to predict agitation?
- How is agitation defined or measured?
- What performance measures are reported?
- What challenges or limitations appear across studies?
- What research gaps remain?

For example, instead of writing:

Study A used a random forest model. Study B used an LSTM model. Study C used a support vector machine.

We can look for a broader pattern:

**Theme:** A wide range of ML and DL models have been explored for agitation prediction, with model selection often depending on the type and structure of the available data.

This gives the reader a better understanding of the research area.
