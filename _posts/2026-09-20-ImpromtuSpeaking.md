Impromptu speaking means speaking spontaneously with very little or no time to prepare.

We use impromptu speaking in many situations. It can happen in daily conversations, Q&A sessions, meetings, interviews, presentations, or social activities.

For example, someone may suddenly ask you:

"What do you think about working from home?"

You need to think and answer immediately.

Impromptu speaking is a learnable skill. It may feel difficult at first, but regular practice can help you become more confident and organised.

<!--more-->

Here are three simple structures that can help.

### Tip #1: What - So What - Now What
This is a simple structure for explaining an idea.

**What?** First, explain what the topic or situation is.

**So What?** Then, explain why it is important.

**Now What?** Finally, explain what we should do next.

For example, if someone asks:

"Why is exercise important?"

You could answer:

- **What**: Exercise is important for our physical and mental health.
- **So What**: It can help us stay healthy and reduce stress.
- **Now What**: Therefore, we should try to include some exercise in our daily routine.

This structure helps you move from the idea, to its importance, and then to an action.

### Tip #2: Problem - Solution - Benefit
This structure is useful when you are asked about a problem or need to suggest an idea.

**Problem:** What is the problem?

**Solution:** What can we do about it?

**Benefit:** What will we get from the solution?

For example:

"How can we improve communication in a team?"

- **Problem**: Sometimes team members do not share information clearly.
- **Solution**: We can have a short team meeting every week to share updates.
- **Benefit**: This can help everyone understand the progress and avoid misunderstandings.

This structure is especially useful in job interviews, meetings, and workplace discussions.

### Tip #3: Point – Reason – Example

This is a useful structure when you need to give your opinion.

**Point**: Give your main answer first.

**Reason**: Explain why you think so.

Example: Give a simple example to support your point.

For example, someone may ask:

"Do you think working from home is a good idea?"

You could say:

- **Point**: Yes, I think working from home can be useful.
- **Reason**: It can give people more flexibility and save commuting time.
- **Example**: For example, I can use the time I normally spend travelling to prepare for work or exercise.

This structure is simple because you only need to remember three things: *Answer → Why → Example*

### Practice Makes It Easier

The most important thing is practice.

You do not need to prepare a perfect answer before speaking. 
Instead, choose a simple question and give yourself 30 seconds to think.

Then speak for one or two minutes.

You can practise with questions such as:

- What is your favourite book?
- Do you prefer working from home or in an office?
- What makes a good leader?
- Should people use AI at work?
- What is one skill everyone should learn?

At first, your answers may not be perfect. That is okay.

The goal is not to speak perfectly.

The goal is to organise your thoughts and keep speaking.

With regular practice, impromptu speaking can become easier and more natural.
