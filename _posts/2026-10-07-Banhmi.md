**BÁNH MÌ** is one of the most famous Vietnamese foods.

It is a Vietnamese sandwich made with a light and crispy baguette. Inside, we can find different types of meat, vegetables, herbs, and sauce.

Bánh mì is simple, affordable, and full of flavour. It is also a good example of how Vietnamese people adapted a foreign food and made it their own.
<!--more-->

### 1. What is BÁNH MÌ?

**Bánh mì** literally means "bread" in Vietnamese.

However, when Vietnamese people say **bánh mì**, they often mean the Vietnamese-style sandwich.

A typical bánh mì has a crispy baguette on the outside and a soft, light inside.

The bread is usually filled with several ingredients, such as:

* Vietnamese pork sausage (**chả lụa**);
* grilled or roasted pork;
* pâté;
* cold cuts;
* fried egg;
* cucumber;
* pickled carrot and daikon;
* coriander;
* chilli;
* mayonnaise or other sauces.

There are many different types of bánh mì.

For example:

**Bánh mì thịt** – a bánh mì with meat.

**Bánh mì trứng** – a bánh mì with egg.

**Bánh mì chả lụa** – a bánh mì with Vietnamese pork sausage.

**Bánh mì xíu mại** – a bánh mì served with Vietnamese-style meatballs.

The ingredients can be different, but the combination of **crispy bread, savoury filling, fresh herbs, and pickled vegetables** gives bánh mì its special taste.

### 2. Origin of BÁNH MÌ

The history of bánh mì is closely connected with the history of Vietnam.

The baguette was introduced to Vietnam during the period of French colonial rule.

At that time, bread was mainly associated with European food.

Over time, Vietnamese people adapted the bread to local tastes and ingredients.

The Vietnamese baguette became lighter and crispier. Local ingredients were also added to create a completely different type of sandwich.

Instead of using only European ingredients, Vietnamese people began adding:

* Vietnamese cold cuts;
* pâté;
* fresh coriander;
* cucumber;
* pickled vegetables;
* chilli;
* Vietnamese sauces.

This created the bánh mì that we know today.

Bánh mì later became popular throughout Vietnam.

It is now common to see bánh mì shops and street stalls in cities and towns across the country.

Bánh mì is also popular outside Vietnam. Vietnamese communities around the world have introduced their own versions of this sandwich.

This is one reason why bánh mì has become an important part of Vietnamese food culture.

### 3. How to make BÁNH MÌ

Making a bánh mì sandwich is quite simple.

The most important part is the bread.

#### Step 1: Prepare the bread

A Vietnamese baguette is made mainly from flour, water, yeast, and salt.

The dough is mixed and allowed to rise.

It is then shaped into small baguettes and baked at a high temperature.

The result should be:

**crispy outside + soft and light inside**

This texture is very important for a good bánh mì.

#### Step 2: Prepare the filling

There are many choices for the filling.

For a traditional bánh mì thịt, we can prepare:

* Vietnamese pork sausage;
* pork cold cuts;
* pâté;
* mayonnaise;
* cucumber;
* coriander;
* pickled carrot and daikon;
* chilli.

The ingredients are usually prepared before the sandwich is assembled.

#### Step 3: Prepare the pickled vegetables

Pickled carrot and daikon are an important part of many bánh mì.

The vegetables are cut into thin strips.

They are then mixed with a simple pickling liquid made from ingredients such as vinegar, sugar, salt, and water.

The vegetables become slightly sour and crunchy.

This gives the sandwich a fresh taste and balances the rich meat and sauce.

#### Step 4: Assemble the sandwich

The baguette is cut open.

A layer of pâté or mayonnaise can be spread inside.

The meat and other fillings are then added.

Finally, add cucumber, pickled vegetables, coriander, and chilli.

Then the bánh mì is ready to eat!

### The secret of a good BÁNH MÌ

A good bánh mì is about **balance**.

The bread should be crispy.

The meat should be savoury.

The vegetables should be fresh and crunchy.

The pickles should be slightly sour.

The herbs should add freshness.

The chilli should add a little heat.

All these simple ingredients come together to create a very tasty sandwich.

### Final Thoughts

Bánh mì is more than just a sandwich.

It is also an example of how food can change when different cultures meet.

The baguette came to Vietnam from France, but Vietnamese people changed it with local ingredients, flavours, and cooking styles.

Today, bánh mì is recognised around the world as a Vietnamese food.

For me, one of the best things about bánh mì is its simplicity.

You do not need expensive ingredients.

A small crispy baguette, some meat, fresh vegetables, herbs, and a good sauce can make a delicious meal.

If you visit Vietnam, try a bánh mì from a local street food shop.

It is cheap, quick, and delicious.

And most importantly, it gives you a small taste of Vietnamese food culture.
